    Model notes: Scan time ≈ TR × NSA × phase steps × (1 + oversampling%) ÷ (SENSE × TSE/turbo factor). TSE/turbo factor is now included (previously excluded entirely, which produced a ~9.5× real-world discrepancy on a TSE cervical axial T2 — est. ~19 min vs an actual ~2 min); it doesn't scale time perfectly linearly in practice and can also affect achievable voxel size, similar to the CS-SENSE factor, so treat this as a major improvement over the prior order-of-magnitude error, not a claim of precise linearity — verify any meaningful TSE factor change at console. Still excludes partial Fourier, prep pulses, and multi-package slice timing. SNR ≈ (1 / (freq × phase)) × √(NSA × phase steps × (1 + oversampling%)) ÷ (g(R) × √R), assuming FOV and slice thickness stay constant between current and proposed — so voxel size is inferred from matrix size only. TR is not part of this SNR number — its effect on signal (via T1 recovery/saturation) is tissue-dependent, so it's called out qualitatively below instead of folded into the percentage. Phase oversampling is modeled like NSA — √(more phase-direction data) — since both just acquire more data in the phase direction, but it doesn't feed voxelFactor: the extra lines extend FOV, not resolution, and are cropped away on recon. Oversampling primarily prevents fold-over/aliasing artifact; the SNR increase is a secondary benefit, not the primary reason to adjust it — don't reduce oversampling below what's needed to prevent aliasing just to save time, even though the calculator will show a time savings for doing so. For CS-SENSE, the g-factor model above is skipped — SNR is instead estimated from the ACQ voxel size you enter directly, as (voxel freq × voxel phase) × √(NSA × (1 + oversampling%)), since CS-SENSE's iterative, variable-density reconstruction isn't something this simple model can approximate — treat those estimates as less precise than standard-SENSE ones, and only compare them against other CS-SENSE estimates (see the "not comparable" note when mixing with standard SENSE). g(R) = 1 + K×(R−1) is a simplified linear g-factor approximation, not derived from actual coil geometry. FOV and Voxel size are a convenience for filling in Matrix (Matrix = FOV ÷ Voxel size, per axis) — only Matrix itself feeds the SNR/time formulas above. This tool produces planning estimates only — always verify against the actual scanner console.

    Reference and checklist only — MAR relies on specialized sequences and technique choices rather than the time/SNR trade-offs the rest of this app models, so nothing here feeds the Calculator. Vendor terminology below follows the scanner vendor selected above; the technique checklist applies regardless of vendor. Checkboxes are just for pulling this up during setup — they reset when you leave the tab.

    Vendor terminology

    View Angle Tilting (VAT) corrects in-plane distortion by tilting the frequency-encoding view angle to match the local field distortion at each slice. Advanced/combined VAT adds SEMAC (Slice Encoding for Metal Artifact Correction) — extra phase-encoding steps in the slice direction — to also correct through-plane distortion. The general MAR suite is the vendor's umbrella toggle bundling these (and related corrections) into one sequence option.
