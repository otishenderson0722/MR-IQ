    Model notes: Scan time ≈ TR × NSA × phase steps × (1 + oversampling%) ÷ (SENSE × TSE/turbo factor). TSE/turbo factor is now included (previously excluded entirely, which produced a ~9.5× real-world discrepancy on a TSE cervical axial T2 — est. ~19 min vs an actual ~2 min); it doesn't scale time perfectly linearly in practice and can also affect achievable voxel size, similar to the CS-SENSE factor, so treat this as a major improvement over the prior order-of-magnitude error, not a claim of precise linearity — verify any meaningful TSE factor change at console. Multi-package slice timing (additional TR passes once slice count exceeds what fits per TR) still isn't modeled — there's no reliable way to estimate max-slices-per-TR with what this app tracks — so entering a slice count flags the time estimate as "Verify at console" instead of silently under-predicting (confirmed ~6× off on an axial brain T2, 24 slices, est. ~19s vs an actual ~2:00). Still excludes partial Fourier and prep pulses (fat sat, IR). GE: phase steps = phase matrix × phase FOV; Acqs (when entered) multiplies time and makes a slice count trustworthy; a bandwidth change scales SNR by √(old ÷ new); switching No Phase Wrap on or off shows "Verify at console". Console time (TA): TR is no longer treated as a time lever at all — real scans showed time doesn't scale with TR when slice count limits it (TR 548 → 400 was predicted at 1:11 and took 2:26). If you enter the console's own time for the Current values, the proposed time is that number scaled only by NSA, phase steps, acceleration factor and oversampling, and any proposal where TR, TSE factor, slice count or a CS-SENSE factor changed shows "Verify at console" instead of a number. SNR ≈ (1 / (freq × phase)) × √(NSA × phase steps × (1 + oversampling%)) ÷ (g(R) × √R), assuming FOV and slice thickness stay constant between current and proposed — so voxel size is inferred from matrix size only. TR is not part of this SNR number — its effect on signal (via T1 recovery/saturation) is tissue-dependent, so it's called out qualitatively below instead of folded into the percentage. Phase oversampling is modeled like NSA — √(more phase-direction data) — since both just acquire more data in the phase direction, but it doesn't feed voxelFactor: the extra lines extend FOV, not resolution, and are cropped away on recon. Oversampling primarily prevents fold-over/aliasing artifact; the SNR increase is a secondary benefit, not the primary reason to adjust it — don't reduce oversampling below what's needed to prevent aliasing just to save time, even though the calculator will show a time savings for doing so. For CS-SENSE, the g-factor model above is skipped — SNR is instead estimated from the ACQ voxel size you enter directly, as (voxel freq × voxel phase) × √(NSA × (1 + oversampling%)), since CS-SENSE's iterative, variable-density reconstruction isn't something this simple model can approximate — treat those estimates as less precise than standard-SENSE ones, and only compare them against other CS-SENSE estimates (see the "not comparable" note when mixing with standard SENSE). Scan time for CS-SENSE isn't estimated at all — dividing by the CS factor like standard SENSE was confirmed ~4x off against actual console time (TR 741, NSA 2, 214×172, CS factor 2.1, TSE factor 7 → this app showed 35.8s, console showed ~150s), and there's no reliable approximation to use instead; the app shows "Verify at console" in its place. g(R) = 1 + K×(R−1) is a simplified linear g-factor approximation, not derived from actual coil geometry. FOV and Voxel size are a convenience for filling in Matrix (Matrix = FOV ÷ Voxel size, per axis) — only Matrix itself feeds the SNR/time formulas above. This tool produces planning estimates only — always verify against the actual scanner console.

    Exam indications — Key vs Supporting

    For each reason an exam is done, mark which sequence categories are Key (they answer the clinical question) and which are Supporting. Key sequences protect quality: tight resolution caps (Cord/nerve for spine and thin T2, Enhancement otherwise), no Aggressive cuts, and a higher-quality option offered even if it costs time. Supporting sequences get Aggressive-level cuts offered by default, with the stronger warning. A protocol is matched to a category from its name (plane, post-contrast, fat-sat, FLAIR, DWI, SWI/GRE, MRA, MRCP, dynamic, in/opposed-phase) and the sequence type; when the plane or contrast isn't in the name it counts as Key if any plausible category is Key. DWI, GRE/SWI, MRA, MRCP and dynamic contrast still show their label and a don't-reduce-quality warning, but the time/SNR model doesn't cover them so no parameters are suggested. Click a cell to flip it; changes save immediately in this browser and are included in Export backup.

    Metal Artifact Reduction (MAR)

    Reference and checklist only — MAR relies on specialized sequences and technique choices rather than the time/SNR trade-offs the rest of this app models, so nothing here feeds the Calculator. Vendor terminology below follows the scanner vendor selected above; the technique checklist applies regardless of vendor. Checkboxes are just for pulling this up during setup — they reset when you leave the tab.

    Vendor terminology

    View Angle Tilting (VAT) corrects in-plane distortion by tilting the frequency-encoding view angle to match the local field distortion at each slice. Advanced/combined VAT adds SEMAC (Slice Encoding for Metal Artifact Correction) — extra phase-encoding steps in the slice direction — to also correct through-plane distortion. The general MAR suite is the vendor's umbrella toggle bundling these (and related corrections) into one sequence option.
